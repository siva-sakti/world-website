# A board's two modes — arrange and edit

**Status: 🟡 the agreed shape, written out for the owner to check — then an antagonist — then
the owner again. Nothing is built.** Written 2026-09-04, from a conversation in which the owner
corrected the two earlier drafts (both archived in `old/`, each with the review that failed it).
**Words are placeholders** — "arrange" and "edit" are the owner's for now; the naming pass may
change them.

**How to read this:** each section says what was **agreed**, quoting the owner where a ruling
was theirs, and marks what is still **⚪ open**. Nothing here is Claude's preference dressed as
a decision. Where Claude proposes something it is marked 🔵.

---

## 1 · Why — two states of mind

**Agreed.** The owner's words, which are the whole reason:

> *"Sometimes you just want to look at your board; sometimes you want to be in the weeds. You
> want to be clutter-free — you don't want to see a lot of busyness when you just want to
> glance at it and get inspired or think about it big picture. But when you edit, you also
> don't want annoyances — you want to just move it around but it starts to edit, or vice
> versa. To me that's pretty annoying."*

So there are two things a person is doing on a board, and they are different:

- **Glancing** — standing back. Big picture. Getting inspired. Composing. You want to move
  things and see the whole, and you want nothing in the way.
- **In the weeds** — working on one thing. Writing in it, naming it, tagging it. You want the
  keyboard and the details, and you do not want to knock anything out of place.

**The surface should match which one you are doing.** Each mode is built by asking one
question — *what is a person DOING here?* — not what they might want. **Arrange is what you do
to what is already there, from a distance. Edit is what makes or changes a thing.** What does
not fit is what gets greyed. That question, not a rule about permissions or data, decides every row of §3.

**The owner's decision, in their words:** *"I think there should be an arrange mode versus an
edit mode. I think it's good. I actually really like the idea."* And, on the cost once it was
laid out honestly: *"that's all we lose — one little extra step to change one button. To me
that's not a big deal."*

**Two things Claude adds, both agreed in conversation:**
- **Switching must be cheap** — one button. The app cannot see your state of mind, only which
  toggle you clicked. If switching is even slightly annoying, the modes become friction instead
  of relief. Owner: *"of course switching is one button."*
- **Nothing is lost.** Every act the board has today stays. The modes **re-categorise** them —
  each one lands in arrange, in edit, or in both. Owner: *"we are just re-categorising, nothing
  is getting lost."*

## 2 · What a board is — the words, plainly

| word | what it is |
|---|---|
| **bit** | one thing you caught or made — a photo, some words, a drawing, a recording, a PDF, a link |
| **board** | a surface you put bits on and give a name |
| **canvas** | one of the two **kinds** of board: no edges, you pan around it |
| **frame** | the other kind: a fixed space, like a page |
| **placement** | the record that one bit is on one board, and where, how big, how tilted |
| **card** | what a placement looks like on screen — not a stored thing |
| **arrange · edit** | the two **modes** a board can be in while you are on it |

### Two choices, at two different times *(owner-ruled 2026-09-04)*

> *"When you start a board it can be one of two kinds, and then once you're in a board it can
> be in one of two modes."*

| choice | when | options | remembered? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **kind** | when you make the board | canvas · frame | yes — it is what the board *is* |
| **mode** | while you are on it | arrange · edit | no — per visit |

Either kind can be in either mode. **The mode belongs to the board you are on**, not to a card,
not to a frame, and not to anything floating above the board (a composition's floater).

> ⚠ **This corrects `frame-plan.md`**, which currently describes the frame as a fixture you
> place *on* a canvas. The owner has ruled it is a *kind of board* chosen at creation. The
> frame plan needs updating before that build; *"moving between canvas and frame more
> smoothly"* is a later question the owner has explicitly deferred.

## 3 · The menu of actions — every one, and where it lives

**This is the heart of the document.** Built from `board-what-you-can-do.md` (the inventory
taken from the code). Each row: what you are trying to do, in a person's words · what we call
it · where it lives · how we are thinking about it — and **⚠ where it does not fit cleanly.**

**The method for every row — use the frame directly, never a proxy.** For each action, ask
*which activity is this part of: glancing at the whole, or working on a thing?* That is an
identity question, and it answers immediately. **Do not translate it** into something easier to
compute — *which table it writes*, *how many clicks it costs*, *whether someone might want it
here*. Each of those was tried in earlier drafts and each gave a plausible wrong answer at the
edges. The owner's frame gives the right one without argument.

*(Owner: "if we straighten out your logic, you should be able to help me make the decisions."
The straightened logic is the sentence above.)*

### 3a · Putting something on the board — **EDIT** *(owner corrected Claude, 2026-09-04)*

> *"This is all composition, or editing. None of this seems like arranging. How would putting
> something new on the board be arranging?"*

**Right.** Claude had these in both, on the reasoning that *getting things in is wanted in either
state*. That was the wrong test. The question is not what you might *want* while glancing — it
is what you are *doing*. **Adding something is composing.** The moment you add, you have stopped
glancing.

**So the line sharpens:** arrange is what you do to *what is already there* — and that includes
taking away. Edit is what *makes or changes a thing* — and that includes putting a new one on.
**In arrange the set of things can shrink. Only in edit can it grow.**

| what you're trying to do | what we call it | arrange | edit | how we're thinking about it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *"start a note right here"* | **new card** *(double-tap empty space)* | 🔘 greyed → the bubble offers edit | ✅ makes it, entered | making a thing is composing. *(The "hands you across" idea is gone — you are already in edit.)* |
| *"draw something"* | **the pen** | 🔘 | ✅ | a drawing is a new thing |
| *"put this photo on"* · paste · drop | **drop · paste** | 🔘 | ✅ *(then the "add a few words?" offer, entering the card)* | |
| *"add a recording / a PDF"* | **+ audio · + pdf** | 🔘 | ✅ | |
| *"paste a link"* | **capture a link** | 🔘 | ✅ | |
| *"bring in that thing I caught yesterday"* | **bring in** *(from the drawer)* | 🔘 | ✅ | owner: *"bringing in from the drawer is also putting something on the page"* |

⚠ **One consequence to notice:** in arrange, **double-tapping empty space does nothing** (or
offers edit). Today it makes a card. That is a third behaviour that changes, added to §7.

### 3c · Looking — **below the modes** *(owner-ruled 2026-09-04, after working it through)*

**Pan, zoom and fit are not acts of either mode. They sit underneath both.** The test that
decided it: a board bigger than the screen. In the weeds, you finish one card and the next is
off-screen — if panning were arrange-only you would switch modes twice to reach it. So panning
must exist in edit, not because it is part of working on a thing but because *you cannot work
on a thing you cannot reach.* They are the precondition for being on the board at all: how you
see, not what you do.

**And this is where the cursor matters most** *(owner)*: *"the difference in the cursor could
help people know which mode they're in, even when they're doing an action that exists in
both."* When you line cards up or type, the act itself tells you the mode. When you pan, it
feels identical in both — **the cursor is the only tell left.** So it is not decoration; it is
the tell for exactly the moments the others go quiet.

| what you're trying to do | what we call it | arrange | edit | how we're thinking about it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *"move around"* | **pan** *(drag empty space)* | ✅ | ✅ | **both, but for a different reason than trash or duplicate:** panning is not part of either activity — it is how you get from one thing to the next. The floor you walk on, not something you do. Same for zoom and fit. **Owner-ruled that panning stays on empty-space drag** — *"wouldn't panning just be dragging your mouse on empty space? Wouldn't that be easier?"* — so select-several takes a modifier or a button, never the plain drag |
| *"closer / further"* | **zoom** | ✅ | ✅ | |
| *"show me everything"* | **fit to view** | ✅ | ✅ | |
| *"when did all this arrive?"* | **timeline** | ✅ | ✅ | a separate page; not affected by mode |

### 3d · Taking things away — and duplicate: **BOTH** *(owner-ruled 2026-09-04)*

> *"Trashing and archiving and removing from a board would make sense to me in the arrange
> mode."* … *"The things I really want to think about are duplicating, trashing, archiving and
> removing from a board — maybe they can be available in both modes, and it's not a big deal."*

**Both, and here is why it is genuinely not a big deal.** The mode split exists for two things:
to stop accidental edits, and to keep the glancing surface clean. **None of these four threatens
either.** Each is one click, confirms before anything destructive, puts no cursor anywhere, and
adds no chrome to a card. Having them in both modes costs nothing the mode was built to protect.
If one feels wrong in practice, greying it is a single change.

| what you're trying to do | what we call it | arrange | edit | how we're thinking about it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *"get this off my board"* | **remove from this board** | ✅ | ✅ | curating is glancing; but in the weeds you may also decide a thing is junk |
| *"put this away for now"* | **archive** | ✅ | ✅ | same |
| *"I'm done with this thing"* | **trash** | ✅ | ✅ | same |
| *"undo that"* | **undo · redo** | ✅ | ✅ | one stack, both modes |

### 3e · Working on one thing *(the four that are edit's own)*

| what you're trying to do | what we call it | arrange | edit | how we're thinking about it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *"write in this"* | **the words** | 🔘 *(click → a bubble offers edit)* | ✅ | in the weeds |
| *"this needs a name"* | **title · caption** | 🔘 | ✅ | |
| *"file this with the retreat stuff"* | **tag** | 🔘 | ✅ | |
| *"where did this come from?"* | **source** | 🔘 | ✅ | |
| *"open it properly"* | **open** *(full page)* | 🔘 greyed | ✅ | going into one thing — whether to write or to read — is the weeds. Owner: *"how would you do that in arrange mode?"* You wouldn't; you switch. *(Claude had it in both for the same wrong reason as §3a: "you might want it here." Same mistake, corrected.)* |

**Edit is small on purpose.** It owns the four things that change what a thing *is*, plus going
into a thing. Everything else it shares with arrange. That is what keeps it from being a bucket.

### 3f · The board itself

| what you're trying to do | what we call it | arrange | edit | how we're thinking about it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *"name this board"* · *"say what it's for"* | **title · description** | ✅ | ✅ | the board's, not a card's |
| *"another arrangement of the same things"* | **duplicate this board** | ✅ | ✅ | |
| *"keep it handy"* · *"file it"* | **pin · group** | — | — | not on the board surface today |

### 3g · ⚠ Where it does not fit cleanly — the rows to look at hardest

1. **Dragging a card in edit.** Owner: *"if you drag across the screen [in edit] it doesn't do
   anything — this type of stuff we need to figure out."* One constraint from the code: **the
   card you are typing in cannot be dragged** — that drag selects text. What dragging *another*
   card does in edit is open. 🔵 Lean: **nothing** — you are in the weeds; things stay put.
   Greying resize and rotate but allowing drag would be inconsistent.
2. **Tagging feels like organising.** *"File this with the retreat stuff"* is the same instinct as
   *"these belong together."* It lives in edit because a tag is about the thing, not this board
   — but it is the row most likely to feel wrong in practice. **Bulk tag** (owner wants it) would
   be a select-several act, which is arrange. ⚪ When bulk tag is built, decide then.
3. **A composition card.** Its "enter" is **already ruled** elsewhere: the floater
   (`docs/composition-spec.md` §10.1.4). This document does not override that. In edit, clicking
   a composition card opens the floater; the board behind it stays in whatever mode it was.
4. **Duplicate in both** is a judgement call, not a rule. If it feels odd in edit, grey it.

## 4 · How you know which mode you're in — the tells *(owner-ruled; section confirmed 2026-09-04)*

The three tells cover different moments: *what happens when you interact* fires only when you
act on something mode-specific · *the toggle* is always there but you have to look · *the
cursor* is always in front of you and needs no act — and it is the only tell during the shared
acts (pan, zoom, fit), where the first goes silent.

> *"Cursor, and then the toggle, and then what happens when you interact — that's the tell."*

Three tells, and **the cards are not one of them**:

| tell | arrange | edit |
|---|---|---|
| **the toggle** — a control with the mode's word on it, always visible | *arrange* | *edit* |
| **the cursor** | 🔵 a grab hand over cards *(this is a thing you pick up)* | 🔵 a text cursor over cards *(this is a thing you go into)* — same over empty space in both, since panning is the same in both |
| **what happens when you interact** | a click selects · a drag moves | a click enters · typing writes |

### The toolbar — one toolbar, greyed by mode *(owner-ruled)*

> *"All the toolbar icons stay the same, but whether you're in one mode or the other some things
> get greyed out or not clickable. That might be more intuitive than shuffling around the whole
> toolbar every time."*

And, earlier: *"it shouldn't be the same toolbar, but a lot of the foundational things should be
the same."* Reconciled as: **the same controls in the same places; what differs is which are
live.** A greyed control is not silent — **tapping it says in one line why, and offers the
switch.** That is the owner's *"helpful on-screen hints and guides for people"* — the greyed
button teaches what the other mode has, and is the shortcut into it.

### The cards — they look like themselves

**Cards look like themselves in both modes.** Owner: *"the cards don't have to look different…
in general I want arrange mode to look like arrange mode, and I want things to look pretty."*

*(For the record: the first idea was stronger — "every time you arrange it's just gonna be a
cleaned-out version of the card, no title, no tag; every time you edit they'd see the details."
The owner then narrowed it themselves: "you want everything you're NOT touching to look like it's
in its final mode." So: clean everywhere, details only on the card you are inside.)*

⚪ **Open:** a subtle interactive hint on cards in arrange — *"little circles on the corners that
get highlighted"* — owner: *"I don't know if we even need that."* Not decided; leave off until
the mode has been lived with.

**The card you are inside, in edit, shows its details** — title, tags, source — and nothing
else on the board changes. *(Owner, earlier: "you want everything you're not touching to look
like it's in its final mode.")* This is not a mode tell; it is just what being inside a card
looks like.

**Edit mode never moves anything.** Owner: *"that would not change how it's arranged on the
page… you'd be able to click in, but you wouldn't be able to move things around."* Same cards,
same places, same zoom. A mode switch is visually calm.

⚠ **One honest exception:** the card you *enter* may **grow** — a text card is as tall as its
content, and entering it adds its title line and formatting bar. **It grows in place and moves
nothing else** (cards are absolutely positioned). So "edit never moves anything" is exactly
true; "nothing changes size" is not, and the earlier draft claimed it was.

### What "enter" means, per kind of card

Clicking a card in edit *enters* it — but a photo has no cursor to place. What entering means,
kind by kind:

| kind of card | click in edit → |
|---|---|
| **text** | the cursor goes into the words; the title line and the formatting bar (bold · italic · list) appear |
| **image · PDF · audio · link** | its caption field is focused; tags and source appear |
| **drawing** | ⚪ open — caption only (🔵 lean), or reopen the pen on it? *(reopening edits saved strokes, which is not built)* |
| **note / composition** | **already ruled elsewhere** — the floater opens (`docs/composition-spec.md` §10.1.4). Not decided here |
| **a board placed as a card** | *(does not render today — nothing to decide)* |

## 5 · Moving between them *(owner-ruled)*

> *"You'll just be in arrange mode until you click into edit, and then you click out."*

- **You land in arrange.** Every time you open a board. Two reasons, both from the trail:
  **safety** — arrange cannot change anything you have made (not a word, not a tag, nothing
  thrown away), so arriving there cannot damage anything: a stray tap on a phone lands harmlessly.
  And **simplicity** — in arrange the entire writing machinery is idle (no save timer, no
  cursor, no title queue), so the mode a board sits in while nobody is doing anything is the
  one with the least running.
- **One button switches.** The toggle. 🔵 Plus a keyboard shortcut.
- **In arrange, clicking a card you've already selected does nothing** — *"if you just click on
  something and you're in arrange mode, I think nothing would happen"* — **except a small bubble
  offers edit:** *"maybe a little bubble can come up saying hey, if you want to go to edit
  mode… a shortcut, in case you forgot."* One tap on the bubble switches with that card ready.
- **Nothing is remembered.** Not per board, not per device, not in the URL.
- **Escape** in edit leaves the card you're in; 🔵 a second Escape returns to arrange.

**What happens to the selection when you switch:**
- arrange → edit with one card selected: that card is entered.
- arrange → edit with several selected: 🔵 the selection clears; nothing entered until you click.
- edit → arrange: the card you were in becomes selected.

## 6 · The unusual situations *(owner: "very, very important")*

Each thought through, because a design that has not met these breaks the first time it does.

| situation | what happens |
|---|---|
| **on a phone — no shift key** | shift-click and shift-drag do not exist. Select-several needs a **touch way**: today that is the select-mode toggle, which this design folds into arrange — so arrange must **keep a way to multi-select by touch**. 🔵 A "select several" button in the toolbar that makes taps toggle selection (which is exactly what the toggle does today, renamed and homed). |
| **on a phone — no hover** | a greyed button cannot explain itself on hover. **It explains on tap**, in one line, and offers the switch. Never silent. |
| **on a phone — the pen** | today the pen refuses finger input (palm rejection for a stylus), so on a phone with no stylus you cannot draw and nothing says why. **Not a mode question** — flagged as its own decision. |
| **keyboard only** | the toggle has a shortcut. In arrange, arrows nudge the selection; Escape clears it. In edit, arrows move the cursor; Escape leaves the card. ⚪ Arrows when nothing is selected in arrange: nudge nothing, or pan? 🔵 pan. |
| **a locked card** | lock is about position. In arrange it does not move. In edit you can still enter it and write. |
| **the pen** | a tool, not a mode. Pick it up in either mode; put it down and you are back where you were. |
| **a composition floating over the board** | the floater has its own input. The board behind it stays in its mode. Mode belongs to the board, not to things above it. ⚪ Confirm with the composition lane before either ships. |
| **two devices on one board** | mode is per device, writes nothing, syncs nothing. No conflict possible. |
| **an empty board** | arrange, with the create doors. There is nothing to be in edit *about* yet. |
| **switching mid-drag** | 🔵 ignored until the drag ends. A mode change cannot land in the middle of a gesture. |
| **the naming pass** | "arrange" and "edit" may change. Every label reads from one place so a rename is one edit. |

## 8 · How it is built *(after the owner and an antagonist have checked §1–§7)*

One value, `mode: "arrange" | "edit"`, replaces the `selectMode` boolean in `board-surface.tsx`.
`editingId` — which card you are inside — stays, and is only ever set in edit.

| file | what changes |
|---|---|
| `board-surface.tsx` | the mode value; landing in arrange; the switch |
| `card.tsx` | a click selects or enters, by mode; the selected-click bubble in arrange; drag per §3g; handles greyed in edit |
| `use-board-pointer.ts` | the select-several behaviour keyed off a toolbar button instead of `selectMode` |
| `use-board-keys.ts` | arrows · Escape · the mode shortcut, by mode |
| `board-toolbar.tsx` | the toggle with its word; greying by mode; greyed-tap explains and offers |
| `selected-bar.tsx` | arrange acts stay; the edit-only four become the entered card's own |

**Not touched:** the database · the save queue · undo · the geometry ledger · `board_cards`.
**No migration. Nothing about your bits changes. Revertible in one commit.**

**Coordination with the composition lane** *(agreed 2026-09-04)*: the one shared file is
`text-bit.tsx`. That lane adds a `variant: 'card' | 'page'` prop (default `'card'`, so every
existing mount is untouched) and exposes formatting-bar visibility as a **`showToolbar` prop**
rather than internal state. **This build keys the bar's visibility off
`variant === 'card' && <the mode gate>` through that prop — it does not own or move the bar
logic.** Their prop lands first in its own small commit; the mode gate rebases onto it.
