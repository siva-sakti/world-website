# The privacy session — prep sheet
*(2026-09-04. Same pattern as the naming prep: everything already true, then the open questions in scenario form — so the sitting starts warm. Nothing here decides; the session is the owner's.)*

## 1 · What is ALREADY BUILT AND TRUE — more than you may remember
- **Everything is private today in practice:** only you can log in; nothing has ever been shown to anyone.
- **But the machinery for guests EXISTS and is proven:** a logged-out visitor could read a thing **only if** it is marked public AND sits live on a public, live board — reachability AND visibility, never either alone (the ruled AND-law, I-P1–P5). No public feed is possible by construction; a private thing renders *absent*, never as a visible hole. Proven leak-proof on a throwaway before it ever went live.
- **The stored defaults, as ruled at different times:** a **bit** is born `public` *(the founding lean)* · a **board** is born `private` *(a whole assembly)* · a **composition** is born `private` *(ruled 2026-09-02)*. ⚠ Note the asymmetry — bits lean open, surfaces lean closed — decided in different eras.
- **A bit's private mark is GLOBAL** — private everywhere it appears, never per-board (ruled, X6).
- **No publish act exists.** The door is open at the database; nothing walks through it.

## 2 · The tension the session exists to resolve
Your founding text: *"new things start public unless I say otherwise… openness is the default."* The built reality: all-private, no way to publish. `philosophy.md` carries the ⚠ banner: **a dedicated session decides — public-by-default, private-by-default, or something smarter.** This is that session.

## 3 · What the composition run parked here
The whole visibility model (§12.2b — controls wanted; inheritance and toggle logic undecided) · **a public piece can have HOLES** (it windows a private bit → the bit renders absent — correct, and it means gaps; §34.4) · what "publish" even does · the publish-preview promise from the founding era (*you're shown exactly what a guest will see before anything is real*).

## 4 · The questions, as scenes *(walk these, rule from feeling)*
1. **The morning page.** You write something raw at 7am. Under each candidate default — what did you have to do (or not do) for it to be safe? Which world do you trust at 7am?
2. **Showing one board to a friend.** You want *one* person to see the retreat board. What do you hand them — a secret link? an account? And what do they see when a private bit sits on that public board (today: it's simply absent)?
3. **The portfolio piece.** A finished composition you're proud of. What's the act that makes it visible, where does it live for a stranger, and what does the preview show you first?
4. **The accidental-leak test.** What's the worst thing that could become visible by a *mistake* under each model — and which mistakes does the model make impossible vs merely unlikely?
5. **The mixed surface.** A public board carrying twenty bits, three private. Is absent-rendering (gaps, no explanation) the right feel — or does the owner's own view need to show her what a guest would miss?
6. **The unmarked bit.** A bit you never touched the visibility of, placed on a board you publish. What happens? *(This is the inheritance question — the single biggest undecided rule.)*
7. **Way downstream, name-only:** what accounts/social/premium would need from this model — a second resident's own notebook (the per-row foundation exists, D-107) · keys handed to lookers (the never-list allows *looking* only) · what could ever be paid-for.

## 5 · The rails already immovable *(rule within these, not against them)*
The never-list: no collaboration, no audience machine, one writer per home · the AND-law (reachable AND visible, never OR) · private renders absent · every publish act deliberate, eyes-open (the preview promise).
