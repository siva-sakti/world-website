# The accounts & social session — running notes
*(The privacy-session pattern: every note numbered, rulings marked, nothing lost. Started 2026-09-06. Prep sheet: `accounts-session-prep.md`. The sealed privacy authority this builds beside: `visibility-model.md`.)*

**N1 — The opening question, hers:** *"What do we even want... why would people want to share? Why do people want things public? That's the question we would want to answer to think about accounts and social."* Goals before machinery, her structure.

**N2 — RULED: no keys.** No person-specific access, no third state between private and public ("a person who can access it who is neither public nor private... seems too tricky"). **Sharing = handing someone the address of a public thing.** Public or private, nothing between. This fills the slot the visibility model parked ("person-keys — accounts era") with a deliberate NO for launch. Re-entry signal if it ever returns: the day she catches herself not publishing something because she only wanted one person to see it.

**N3 — RULED: the collapse.** Only public things can be shared; a share is publish + a URL. Zero new model — the sealed two switches carry the whole weight. (Claude's read: this matches the model exactly; Google-Docs-style person-sharing earns its keep there because Docs is collaborative editing, and this world is one writer per home, visitors look only.)

**N4 — The five whys (the menu, from her recorded words):** ① to be read · ② to give (quotes, bits, boards) · ③ to be known (profiles) · ④ to get feedback · ⑤ to have an audience. The ladder of reader powers: ①② readers are eyes (no reader accounts) → ③ owners get a face → ④ readers get a voice (accounts + moderation enter) → ⑤ readers get a relationship (the attention loop). Each rung a distinct pile of machinery — which is how this question scopes accounts vs social.

**N5 — Her responses to the whys:** ①② implicitly yes (her own recorded wants). ③ **leaning yes** — *"Are.na has profiles, you can go to someone's page. I believe it would be nice for us to have that."* ④ reframed by her: her "feedback" want = **suggestion mode inside compositions** (Google-Docs-style suggesting), *"later down the line, not the most priority"* → filed F-10. ⑤ open — *"this is a personal tool, but it has a social edge... curious how to think about it"* — the social sitting's centerpiece.

**N6 — Paid, her want:** *"We're gonna have paid stuff. If we can think about how to make people pay for storage and all, I would like that."* Verified precedents (2026-09-06): **Notion sells version-history retention by tier** (auto-snapshot every 10 min while editing; free keeps 7 days, Plus 30, Business 90, Enterprise unlimited). **Are.na sells block caps** (free = 200 blocks total; Premium $7/mo or $70/yr = unlimited + power features). Both cousins monetize storage/history — her instinct has working precedents. Feeds prep scene 6.

**N7 — Versioning research (verified 2026-09-06, sources in the session log):** Google Docs runs **two systems at once**: an automatic revision trail (continuous, older revisions get merged/collapsed over time) AND up to 40 **named versions** minted on purpose, kept unmerged; restore or copy-out. Notion: automatic snapshots only (10-min cadence), retention by plan tier. The pattern: **the safety net** (automatic, time-limited, answers "I broke it yesterday") and **milestones** (deliberate, kept, answers "the version I sent them") are different jobs. Her fear — *"I don't want people to feel frustrated that things were lost"* — is the safety-net job; F-8's "forks are not history" + link-to-a-version is the milestone job.

**N8 — The freeze problem, explained (she asked "what are we freezing?"):** a composition = typed words stored in the piece + quoted bits stored as *pointers* ("show bit #123 here"). Copying the body freezes the words but not the bits — an old version would show yesterday's prose with today's bit-content inside. A **true snapshot** copies what each quoted bit showed at mint time into the version (the spec's export rule already works this way: "an export freezes what it showed").

**N9 — Versions × privacy, the one-sentence closure (proposed, not yet ruled):** **a version has no switch of its own — it rides its piece's switch.** Unpublish the piece and every version's link goes dark with it; a version is never more public than its piece. With true snapshots, a version's bit-copies are disclosures chosen at mint time (exactly how the resolve list already treats "copy"), so flipping the original bit private later doesn't reach into them — no leak through history, no new states.

**N10 — The versioning stance on the table (Claude's proposal, awaiting her):** share-links point at the living thing by default · a version is minted on purpose (publish being a natural auto-mint moment) · a minted version is a true snapshot · versions ride the piece's switch. Plus, separately and cheaper: a Notion-style time-limited auto-safety-net if "nothing is ever lost" should be a promise. F-8 stays sequenced post-migration, after living with compositions.
