**N4 — The five whys (the menu, from her recorded words):** ① to be read · ② to give (quotes, bits, boards) · ③ to be known (profiles) · ④ to get feedback · ⑤ to have an audience. The ladder of reader powers: ①② readers are eyes (no reader accounts) → ③ owners get a face → ④ readers get a voice (accounts + moderation enter) → ⑤ readers get a relationship (the attention loop). Each rung a distinct pile of machinery — which is how this question scopes accounts vs social.

**N6 — Paid, her want:** *"We're gonna have paid stuff. **N7 — Versioning research (verified 2026-09-06, sources in the session log):** Google Docs runs **two systems at once**: an automatic revision trail (continuous, older revisions get merged/collapsed over time) AND up to 40 **named versions** minted on purpose, kept unmerged; restore or copy-out. Notion: automatic snapshots only (10-min cadence), retention by plan tier. The pattern: **the safety net** (automatic, time-limited, answers "I broke it yesterday") and **milestones** (deliberate, kept, answers "the version I sent them") are different jobs. Her fear — *"I don't want people to feel frustrated that things were lost"* — is the safety-net job; F-8's "forks are not history" + link-to-a-version is the milestone job.

**N8 — The freeze problem, explained (she asked "what are we freezing?"):** a composition = typed words stored in the piece + quoted bits stored as *pointers* ("show bit #123 here"). Copying the body freezes the words but not the bits — an old version would show yesterday's prose with today's bit-content inside. A **true snapshot** copies what each quoted bit showed at mint time into the version (the spec's export rule already works this way: "an export freezes what it showed").

**N9 — Versions × privacy, the one-sentence closure (proposed, not yet ruled):** **a version has no switch of its own — it rides its piece's switch.** Unpublish the piece and every version's link goes dark with it; a version is never more public than its piece. With true snapshots, a version's bit-copies are disclosures chosen at mint time (exactly how the resolve list already treats "copy"), so flipping the original bit private later doesn't reach into them — no leak through history, no new states.

**N10 — The versioning stance on the table (Claude's proposal, awaiting her):** share-links point at the living thing by default · a version is minted on purpose (publish being a natural auto-mint moment) · a minted version is a true snapshot · versions ride the piece's switch. Plus, separately and cheaper: a Notion-style time-limited auto-safety-net if "nothing is ever lost" should be a promise. F-8 stays sequenced post-migration, after living with compositions.

**N13 — The corner's three live options, laid out for her ruling.** The colliding invariants: *never edit her prose* × *never block a privacy flip*. **A — unpublish the piece (sealed):** prose intact, piece leaves public view, fully reversible; cost = a published piece quietly goes dark. **B — remove the quote (her instinct 2026-09-06):** piece stays public; cost = the app edited her writing and the surrounding sentence breaks. **C — hole + loud repair flag (the mid-session ruling):** prose untouched, piece stays public; cost = a visitor may see the hole in the repair window — the exact thing the composition law exists to prevent. Dead with reasons intact: *block the flip* (violates never-block) · *degrade the quote to plain text* (leaks the private bit's own words). **Claude's recommendation: A** — the only option where nothing bad is visible to anyone (B damages her writing, C shows a stranger a hole); A's cost is only that something stops being visible, which is the direction her own act asked for; and A alone is fully reversible. **If A's sting is "quietly gone," the fix is a screen not a rule:** the piece's own page carries a visible *"unpublished when you made [bit] private — republish?"* state, turning a disappearance into a to-do. **AWAITING HER WORD — she asked to sit with it; everything else in the sealed model stands meanwhile.**

**N14 — RULED-ish (hers, Claude concurring): accounts does NOT change the visibility model.** *"I guess it doesn't change necessarily visibility work."* Correct, and the reason is structural: every visitor rule is an AND over two switches — *is the thing public* × *is the surface public* — and **neither asks who is looking.** Many residents = many worlds each running the identical model; the database already carries per-row ownership. **What accounts DOES change is what "public" is worth:** today public = permitted-but-unreachable (no feed, no profile, no directory — stage 2); once profiles exist, public things become **findable by strangers** and stage 3 (OUT) becomes real for everything at once. **The sequencing rule this yields: the companions ship BEFORE profiles** — view-as-visitor, the marker, the review screens are what make publishing safe to do casually, and they matter far more once public means findable. Building profiles first would be the mistake.
