## 3 · The open questions, with her reactions *(2026-09-04)*

### 3.1 ⭐ "bit" × "piece" — ONE decision about a PAIR
**Her catch:** *bit* and *piece* are synonyms in plain English (*a bit of paper / a piece of paper*) — currently naming the app's SMALLEST and LARGEST things. **She opened the small end herself:** *"maybe we change the word bit… I'm totally open to renaming this."*
**Her verdicts on the ranges so far:** ✅ likes **clipping** (*"very artistic and aesthetic — you're taking a clipping"*) · ✅ likes **find** *(with a note: reads as a verb)* · 🟡 **scrap** (*"a little too drafty, too unrefined"*) · ❌ **card** · 🟡 **capture** (*"sounds like a verb"*).
**Fuller ranges, for her reactions:**
- *the clipping family:* clip · clipping · **cutting** · snippet · cutout · trimming
- *the find family:* find · a keep · **a catch** · haul · gleaning · pickup
- *the studio family:* **swatch** · specimen · sample · fragment · morsel · token · ephemera · marginalia
- **Two observations worth her eye:** ⭐ **"cutting"** is both a newspaper cutting AND a gardening cutting — the snipped piece you root to grow a new plant; `philosophy.md` is literally about growing ideas from what you caught. ⭐ **"catch" is already her verb** (*"catch what you consume"*, philosophy.md) — so *"a catch"* is a noun she has half-invented already; weak plural.
**The big end:** ✅ she likes **piece** or **document** · ⛔ **not "page"** — *her catch:* the board's frame view is already a page of sorts · 🟡 **composition** — *"sounds very formal"*.
**⭐ The test that decides it is the PAIR, said aloud:** *"three ___ in this ___"*.

### 3.2 "gather" — she split a job Claude had collapsed
*"It's more of an action that has to feel smoother than a verb people are saying… but it helps if we can define a verb and make it a thing."* → **two jobs:** the **gesture** (`[[`, the drawer click — mostly invisible, may need no word) vs the **relationship** the app must SHOW (the backlink line, the drawer tab, "___ into" — this one needs a word).
Her leanings: ✅ *"I like pull or gather"* · ❌ *bring* (*"that's what you're doing, but it's not defining a thing"*). Range: gather · pull · draw · call · fetch. **Backward** today reads *"gathered into"*.

### 3.3 ⭐ The two surfaces — her question, and the analysis it produced
*"Let's think about the different surfaces — maybe that helps with naming. What does each give you? When would you use each?"*
| | **a piece floating over a BOARD** | **bits propped over a PIECE** |
|---|---|---|
| the ground | the board — material, spread | the writing — the whole surface |
| the visitor | a small writing window | small propped-up things |
| attention | on the **arrangement**; writing emerges from it | on the **sentence**; material is reference |
| when | the board's grown for weeks; you start the through-line while looking at it | you're drafting and need that one quote in your eyeline |
| the analog | writing at a desk covered in spread-out material | **a music stand beside the page** |
⭐ **One is *writing while looking*; the other is *looking while writing*.** Opposite postures — **they should probably NOT share a family name.**
**On the second:** what it achieves is *keeping something in sight while your hands are busy elsewhere* — visibility during work, never storage. ⛔ **not "corkboard"** — that is the BOARD (things live there); this is **propping** (the open book face-down, the photo clipped to the lamp, the music stand). Her own instinct: ✅ **call it a MODE** — *"like you're in the study"* — which fits: a posture you enter, not an object you place.

### 3.4 ⚠ "block" — a CONFIRMED defect (owner, 2026-09-04)
*"Anything can be a block on the composition surface — it's just that some blocks are bits, or links to other compositions."* **Right:** a paragraph is a block, a heading is a block — so "block" cannot ALSO name a pulled-in thing. **What is actually distinct is provenance, not blockness:** its content lives elsewhere. ✅ **"chip" survives** (*"chip is kind of cute"*); the SHOWN form needs the precise word. *(Marked at the definition in `composition-spec.md` §3.3.)*

## 5 · ✅ Confirmed by the owner (2026-09-04) — the board family
*(Her words, verbatim. **Recorded, not enacted** — she said "not to action on"; no code sweep, no lexicon graduation, until she calls it.)*

- **board** — the surface you make and name *(stored)*
- **canvas** — the board's infinite spatial rendering. How a board is **drawn**, not a thing you have *(not stored)*
- **frame** — an optional page-shaped fixture placed on a board. The board stays infinite; the frame sits within it *(stored, when built)*
- **arrange / edit** — modes of the board *(not stored)*

**Two things these four expose — flagged, not solved:**

### 5.1 ⚠ "frame" is about to be stored TWICE, meaning two different things
Both are planned and neither is built, so this is cheap to fix now and expensive later.
| | what it means | where it would live |
|---|---|---|
| **frame** *(confirmed above)* | the page-shaped fixture on a board | `board.frame_x/y/w/h` — `frame-plan.md` §33 *(+ a future `frame` table, §52)* |
| **frame** *(already planned)* | a **card's border treatment**: none · paper-mat · hairline · polaroid · museum | `placement.frame` — `aesthetics-phase.md` §54, P3 §102 |
One word, two stored things, one screen. **One of them needs a different word** — and the card-border sense is the easier one to move, since nothing has been built on it and its own doc already calls it "border/frame."

### 5.2 ⭐ "mode" is now carrying two different jobs
Her own definitions separate them cleanly, which is the useful part:
- **how a board is DRAWN** — she calls this a *rendering* (canvas). But `lexicon.md` also has **collection mode** and **document mode** on this same axis, and calls canvas "a board's spatial rendering **mode**."
- **what your HANDS are doing** — arrange / edit. This is the axis she just called *mode*.
- and §3.3's propping surface is a third thing she also wants to call **a mode** (*"like you're in the study"*).

**If "canvas is a rendering, not a mode" holds, it settles the other two for free:** collection and document become *renderings* alongside canvas, and **mode** is left to mean only *what your hands are doing* — which is exactly the sense §3.3 reaches for. Not ruled; her call.
