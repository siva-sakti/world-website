## 2b · THE NAME HUNT — first seeds (the owner opened it, 2026-08-31)

**The owner's seed, verbatim:** *"Are.na is in the world I like, because it's related to the big space. But yeah, I want it to feel more creative — like otherworldly."*

**The insight that fell out** 🔵: "otherworldly" is not an aesthetic preference — **it's structurally true of the product.** The extended-mind framework (`product-concept-frameworks.md` §2) says thinking happens out in space; the app IS an other world in the literal sense — **the world of your mind, made into a place you can walk into.** So the name's candidate job: **a proper noun for the world that is you, outside you.** This also explains why "world" has always felt right-but-flat — correct *category*, unmarked *word*.

**Are.na's mechanism, decomposed** (steal the mechanism, never the name):
1. **a space word** — arena, the big open place (the owner's own observation);
2. **a fracture that makes it strange** — "arena" is sports; "are.na" is a place you've never been.
→ **common space word + one twist of strangeness = a real place that doesn't exist.** "Otherworldly" can cost one letter; it doesn't require an alien word.

**Tests a candidate must pass** (from how people actually use Are.na's name):
- the possessive: *"it's in my ___"* sounds natural
- a place you're **in**, not a tool you **use**
- short, sayable, spellable on hearing

**Seed directions** 🔵 *(raw material only — ⚑ the owner is the writer; reject freely)*:
- **space words made strange:** terra · atlas (a book of worlds — the owner its only cartographer) · aether · orbit · monde / mundus
- **otherworld words:** elsewhere (as a proper noun — "in my Elsewhere") · reverie · the interior
- **from the owner's own interests** *(the direction that feels most hers)*: astrology → **the houses** (the twelve domains of one life under one sky — exactly the multi-mind's many interests in one home) · constellation (already in the board-seed list)
- **the concept-word to know even if never the name:** *umwelt* — biology's term for **the world as experienced by one particular mind**. Semantically a bullseye; as a sound, maybe too heavy. But it names the thing the name is trying to name.

**Where this fits the run plan:** the naming *decision* stays sitting ⑦ (after publishing + the argument settles). This section **accumulates seeds passively until then** — add one-liners any time an idea strikes; nothing here commits to anything.

## 3 · Why this is blocked, and on what

Brand isn't just unstarted — it's **downstream of two unanswered questions**:

1. **Privacy / publishing** (`product-concept-privacy.md`). If the app has no public face and sharing means handing one trusted person a key, then "brand" means something much smaller than a landing page and a mark. **Decide that first.**
2. **The promise** (`product-concept-promise.md`). A name and a voice are compressions of the argument. Naming before the argument settles produces a name for the wrong thing.

⚠ **This is also why the naming questions in `product-concept-language.md` matter here:** `note`→`composition` and *is `board` right* are the app's **internal** vocabulary. The app's **own name** is a separate question and shouldn't get bundled into that sweep.

## 4 · Open questions

1. ⚪ **What is this called?** "world" is a placeholder.
2. ⚪ **Does it need a public face at all?** — depends entirely on privacy/publishing.
3. ⚪ **Is the in-app aesthetic the brand**, or does the outward identity differ? (*Japanese · ethereal · analog* is a real position — it may already be the answer.)
4. ⚪ **Who is the audience for the brand** — the owner, the "Are.na crowd" the owner named, or a wider creative-practice audience?
5. ⚪ **Does the "does everything" framing help or hurt?** The owner noticed themselves that *home base for a creative practice* is drifting toward a brand claim. Broad claims are hard to make feel specific and beautiful.
