# The joints, as scenes — how it's actually used

> ## STATUS · 2026-09-02 · 🟠 WORKING — the sitting's material
> The owner's unfinished list: confident on the composition itself; **not confident on the joints** — composition↔bit, composition↔board, the bit's larger role, *"how will people actually use this, what do they want to see."* The abstract connection questions bounced three times; so here they are **as moments of use**, each with its open choice ⚑ embedded where it can be *felt*, not argued. The sitting = walking these together; the owner corrects the scenes from life, then answers the ⚑ inside each.

---

## S1 · The quote, mid-sentence *(the settled happy path — the baseline)*
You're writing. You remember a line you saved from an article weeks ago. You type `[[`, three letters of it, pick it — a chip lands in your sentence, carrying its source. Done; never left the writing.
**Everything here is ruled and mostly built.** This is the joint working as designed.

## S2 · The pitch built from last year's pitch *(composition→composition — ruled yes; the SEEING side open)*
You start this year's pitch. You want last year's beside you. You `[[` it — a chip lands, styled as a piece, not a scrap.
⚑ **The open choice, felt from the other side:** months later you open *last year's* pitch. Should its page tell you *"pulled into: this year's pitch"*? A quiet line at the bottom, like a bit's? Something more? Nothing?
⚑ And when you tap the chip — peek in place, or open in a floater?

## S3 · Writing beside the board *(composition↔board — mostly ruled; the card open)*
You're on your album board. You hit compose — a floater opens over the canvas. You pull two bits in from the drawer while looking at everything. You close it. A card for the piece now sits on the board.
⚑ **The open choice:** what does that card show? Just the title? The title and the opening lines? Can you stretch it to read more? *(This is the mock — but feel it here first: you return to this board next week. What do you WANT to see of the piece?)*

## S4 · The bit that keeps coming back *(the bit's larger role — the seeing side)*
One quote, pulled into three different pieces across four months. You land on the bit's page.
Today it shows: the quote, its source, its tags, the boards it sits on, and "gathered into" — the three pieces.
⚑ **The open question:** is that the right picture of a bit's life? Anything missing you'd want — when each pull happened? Nothing more? *(This page IS "what a bit is for," made visible.)*

## S5 · Two bits that belong together, and no board wants them *(THE bit↔bit question, as a moment)*
You're browsing. An old jot — a half-thought about attention — suddenly answers a screenshot you saved last month. They belong together. No board fits them; making a board for two things feels absurd.
**What do you do?** Today's honest options: tag them both with a shared word · or write a two-sentence text bit — *"this jot answers that screenshot"* — and `[[` both into it (works today; the connection carries its why).
⚑ **The choice:** is the tiny connective note the RIGHT answer (a tie that explains itself) — or does this moment want a lighter gesture, a bare "these relate" with no words? **Feel this one hard: it decides the bit-web.**

## S6 · The one sentence you actually need *(excerpt — restored from the audit)*
You saved a long article as one bit — your rule: capture messy, one grab. Now you want ONE sentence of it in your piece. `[[` pulls the whole thing.
⚑ **The choice:** what should exist here? Pull-then-trim in place? A way to lift the sentence out as its own bit (keeping its parent and source)? Or is pulling the whole and quoting by hand fine? *(For PDFs and voice memos this is sharper still — today they can't be quoted at all.)*

## S7 · The piece that knows its job *(frame — your top-flagged idea, as a moment)*
You start a pitch. It must be under 300 words, for a specific editor, by Friday.
Today the piece knows none of that — every composition is an identical blank.
⚑ **The ideation, live:** what would you want the piece to hold about its own job? A word target you can see against the count? A "for" line? A deadline that surfaces somewhere? Or is this all just the first line of the writing itself — no feature at all?

## S8 · The project, seen whole *(board↔compositions, from above)*
Your album board holds forty bits — and, by now, three placed pieces. You come back after two weeks and want to see *the writing of this project*.
⚑ **The open question:** is looking at the board enough (three cards among forty)? Or do you want some way to see a board's pieces as a set? *(Maybe nothing — the board IS the view. Feel it.)*

---

**How the sitting works:** walk S1→S8 in order. For each: first correct the scene itself ("that's not how I'd move"), then answer its ⚑. By S8, the joints are ruled from use — and the answers flow into `composition-definition.md` §4 and the base spec.
