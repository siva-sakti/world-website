# The owner's workshopping, 2026-09-04 — findings before words
*(Her framing: "I'm actually very open to workshopping all of this." Recorded as findings + ranges; still no proposals from Claude — the pen is hers.)*

## F1 · ⭐ "piece" vs "bit" is a COLLISION, and it may be BIT that gives
**Her catch:** *"my problem with piece is it sounds like a small thing… bit and piece are synonyms in many people's minds."* **She is right in plain English** (*a piece of paper / a bit of paper*) — two synonyms for "small fragment" naming the app's SMALLEST and LARGEST things.
⭐ **And she opened the other end herself:** *"is there something better than bit?"* — worth taking seriously: "bit" was named when it was the app's only unit, and D-ruled since that a bit may be a whole PDF, so the word's smallness is already under strain from its own side.
**Therefore: this is ONE decision about a PAIR, not two decisions.** Two levers: rename the big thing, or rename the small thing and let "piece" take the big slot.
**The range that exists in the world** *(vocabulary, not proposals)* — small: clipping · scrap · find · capture · card · cutting. Large: piece · draft · essay · document · page · writing. **"Composition"** — her own read: *"sounds very formal."*

## F2 · "gather" — the owner split a job Claude had collapsed
**Her distinction:** *"it's more of an action that has to feel smoother than a verb that people are saying… but I think it helps if we can define a verb and make it a thing."* **Two naming jobs, not one:**
1. **the gesture** — what the hands do (`[[`, the drawer click). Mostly invisible; nobody narrates it. **May need no word at all.**
2. **the relationship** — the noun the app must SHOW: the backlink line, the drawer tab, "___ into". **This one genuinely needs a word.**
**Ranges:** verb — gather · pull · bring · draw · call · fetch. Noun for what a piece has collected — *no obvious English winner exists*, which is itself a finding.

## F3 · The hover layer — the owner asked the right question first
*"What is this actually achieving, and what is the analog-world analog?"*
**What it achieves:** keeping something IN SIGHT while your hands are busy elsewhere — visibility during work; not storage, not arrangement.
**The analog is NOT a corkboard** — that is the BOARD (things live there). It is what a person PROPS UP while working: the open book laid face-down beside you · a photo clipped to the desk lamp · a note under a paperweight · **the music stand**.
⭐ **The naming test that falls out: the right word describes PROPPING, not STORING.**

## F4 · "block" is imprecise — the owner caught it
*"Anything can be a block on the composition surface… it's just that some blocks are bits or links to other compositions."* **Correct, and a real defect in the current vocabulary:** a paragraph IS a block, a heading IS a block — so "block" cannot also be the name of a pulled-in thing.
**What is actually distinct is provenance, not blockness:** it is a block whose CONTENT LIVES ELSEWHERE (§24.5b's window). ⚑ **The lexicon's "block" entry (§3.3, already flagged "overloaded — naming session item") is now a confirmed defect, not a suspicion.** ✅ **"chip" survives her stamp** (*"chip is kind of cute"*); the SHOWN form needs the precise word.
