## 1 · The method premise 🟢

> **needs → flows → features. In that order.**

Features are **disposable**. *"I'm actually happy to build and rebuild as long as I feel like the core, the argument, meaning our value-add, is very clear."* So the anchor is the argument, never the feature list. Anything here that turns out wrong gets rewritten and the code follows.

## 2 · What the app is actually for 🟢 *(the claim)* / 🔵 *(the phrasing)*

**The app is not for making the artifact.** The DAW, the canvas, the camera, the word processor already own that. It's for **everything around the making** — the scattered surround of a creative life.

The owner's point, which they flagged as core: *"there's a lot more to the creative process than just producing. There's a lot a creative person needs to organize."* A visual artist chases exhibitions, writes a statement, emails people. A writer researches which publications to pitch. A musician has melodies, song ideas, and an outfit for the show.

**The framing, in the owner's words** 🟢 *(2026-08-30 — corrected away from Claude's version):* **"we accommodate for things that are other parts of the creative process."** ⛔ Claude proposed *"everything you have to hold in your head that isn't the thing you actually want to be doing"* — the owner **half-rejected** it: it smuggles in a judgment about which parts of a practice you enjoy. Use the neutral phrasing.

**The six areas** 🔵 *(my list, from the owner's examples — ⚪ still unchecked as the right cut; the owner has warned before against bolting categories onto a simple idea):*

| area | what lives there | who owns it today |
|---|---|---|
| **material** | references, song ideas, melodies, quotes, images, clips | Are.na · Pinterest · camera roll · Readwise |
| **the work** | drafts, arrangements, studies, sketches | the production app |
| **meaning** | the artist statement, the concept, *what is this and why* | **nobody** — a notes app |
| **outreach** | who to email, pitches, submissions, collaborators | **nobody** — email drafts, a spreadsheet |
| **opportunities** | publications, galleries, grants, deadlines, open calls | **nobody** — bookmarks, a Notion table |
| **presentation** | the exhibition, the setlist, the outfit, the run of show | **nobody** |

### ⭐⭐ THE BETTER CUT — REQUIRED ARTIFACTS, not abstract areas (2026-08-31)

**Where it came from.** The owner, watching a video: *"if you're new to making art and a gallerist asks you for a list of work — how do you make that PDF? There's a lot of stuff like this."* Plus the honest limit: *"I don't know about nuances in the creative world. I can get the things and patterns."*

**The reframe:** every discipline requires **documents you must produce that are not your art.** Concrete, checkable, and instantly recognisable to the person who has to make one.

| discipline | required artifacts |
|---|---|
| **artist** | list of work · artist statement · CV · exhibition proposal |
| **writer** | pitch · query letter · bio · synopsis |
| **musician** | one-sheet · EPK · bio · setlist · liner notes |
| **designer** | portfolio · case study · deck |
| **filmmaker** | treatment · shot list · pitch deck |

**⚠ CORRECTION (owner, same day):** Claude first wrote *"artifacts beat the six areas."* Over-corrected. The owner: *"I do think the six areas are helpful to start thinking about this — what I'm trying to do is drill down, which is what we're doing now. I don't wanna discard one or the other."*

> **They are two levels of one thing. The six areas are the CATEGORIES; the artifacts are the INSTANCES inside them.** A *list of work* is an outreach/presentation artifact. An *artist statement* is a meaning artifact. **Areas organise the thinking; artifacts make it concrete and recognisable.**

Use areas to reason and check coverage; **use artifacts whenever a person is being spoken to** — an artist reading "list of work" feels seen instantly; nobody feels seen by "presentation." And the pairing is what gives the research its grid (`product-concept-frameworks.md` §Q7).

**It hands us the onboarding line, per discipline** (the owner's own phrasing): ***"You're an artist. You have to make a list of work. Here's how this app does that."*** — the switching pitch (§Are.na), made concrete one discipline at a time.

**And it produces features directly rather than by inference.** A list of work = rows with fields (title · year · medium · dimensions) → **structured data** (`tables-and-structured-data.md`) · assembled from finished work → **pieces** (§Finding 3) · handed to someone → **an output artifact / export**. Three open threads converging on one buildable, demoable thing.

⚠ **THE METHOD WARNING, and it is not optional.** The owner is the oracle for **one** discipline. For every other one, Claude will generate *plausible* workflows that no practitioner would recognise — convincing fiction we would then build features on. **Two honest sources only: real accounts (research, practitioner writing, video) and real people who can be asked.** Never invention. → this changes the research round, see `product-concept-frameworks.md` §5.

### ⭐⭐ THE SORT — internal vs external, and the REDUCTION (owner, 2026-08-31)

The owner: *"There's a couple components here — there's the core surfaces, the internal component, and I keep giving you external stuff too, so I want to sort that in the right place."* And on discipline knowledge: for onboarding, *"a gallerist wants you to have this — if you're an artist you can just have this easy workflow,"* but **for building features, "I don't think it's as relevant."**

| | **EXTERNAL** — onboarding · demo · the pitch | **INTERNAL** — what we build |
|---|---|---|
| the question | *"You're an artist. A gallerist asks for a list of work. Here's how."* | *"What generic thing IS a list of work?"* |
| needs | discipline knowledge · real artifacts · real names | **surfaces and formats** |
| how many | as many as there are disciplines | **as few as possible** |
| lands in | level 2 — journeys · onboarding | level 1 — the feature set |

> **We never build a "list of work" feature. We build the generic thing it reduces to.** Discipline knowledge is demo material.

### The reduction — every artifact → a generic type

| generic type | the artifacts that ARE it | have it? |
|---|---|---|
| **prose document** | artist statement · bio · pitch · query letter · treatment · synopsis · liner notes | ✅ **composition** |
| **spatial arrangement** | vision board · moodboard · shot board · album board | ✅ **board** |
| **a table with fields** | gallery tracker · submission tracker · **list of work** · contact list | ❌ **structured data** → `tables-and-structured-data.md` |
| **an ordered sequence** | pitch deck · setlist · track order · shot list · talk points | ❌ **sequence** → §2d |
| **a file you hand someone** | the list-of-work **PDF** · portfolio · EPK · one-sheet | ❌ **an OUTPUT ARTIFACT — new, 2026-08-31** |

> **Five generic types. We have two.**

**The new one: an output artifact.** Something that *leaves the app as a file* for a person outside it. Surfaced first by the owner's gallerist/PDF example, and it generalises — every discipline's required artifacts eventually get handed over. Distinct from **export** (`/api/export` = your whole world as JSON, for ownership) and distinct from **publishing** (a live surface someone visits). This is *one document, made to be sent*. ⚑ Unruled, unnamed, unbuilt. ⚠ It also touches **pieces** (§Finding 3 — what you'd hand over is usually finished work).

**Consequence for the research round:** this **downgrades** per-discipline research from blocking to level-2. If the feature set only needs the five generic types, discipline knowledge serves the **demo and onboarding**, not the build. *(Claude had ranked it as load-bearing an hour earlier — corrected.)*

### Each area, at length — because the table flattens what's actually going on

**Material.** The one area that *is* well served — and even here it's fragmented across four tools that don't talk to each other. The screenshots are in the camera roll. The saved images are in Are.na. The quotes are in the notes app. The melodies are in voice memos. A person doesn't experience this as "I have good tools for material"; they experience it as *"I know I saved that somewhere."* So even the served area has an unserved version of the problem: **not capture, but reunion.**

**The work.** Owned by the production app — Ableton, Photoshop, Final Cut, the word processor — and *rightly so*. We are not competing here and shouldn't. But there's a break worth naming: the work-in-progress usually lives somewhere entirely separate from the material it came from. The reference photo that started the painting isn't in Photoshop. The article that sparked the essay isn't in the doc. **The break between material and work is the first seam, and it's the one people feel most often without naming it.**

**Meaning.** Every artist eventually has to say what the thing *is* — an artist statement, a concept, a description for a grant application, an "about" paragraph. Nobody has a home for this. It's typically a Google Doc, rewritten from scratch every time, disconnected from both the work it describes and the last four versions of itself. **This is the area where a composition is most obviously the answer**, and where the writer's-tool worry dissolves: a painter needs to write here too, and hates it precisely because nothing supports it.

**Outreach.** Who to email, what to pitch, who you already contacted, what you said last time. Currently: draft emails, a spreadsheet if you're organised, memory if you're not. The specific pain is that **the pitch is disconnected from the thing it's pitching** — you write "here's my work" in a mail client that has never seen your work.

**Opportunities.** Which publications, galleries, grants, residencies, open calls; when they close. Currently scattered across bookmarks, a Notion table, an Instagram save, a newsletter you meant to read. **This is the most list-shaped area of the six** — and it's the one that produced the tables idea (`tables-and-structured-data.md`). It's also the most deadline-driven, which is why *a date you set* keeps surfacing as the one missing field.

**Presentation.** The exhibition layout, the setlist, the run of show, the outfit. **The most under-recognised of the six** — most people wouldn't call it creative work, and it absolutely is. The owner's own Sruthi seed put *the outfit she wants to wear while performing* right beside the song drafts, which is exactly the instinct: **it's part of the work, so it belongs with the work.** There is no tool for this whatsoever.

**The making is one slice of six. Four of the other five have no home at all.** That's the opening — *the everything-else*.

### The line that keeps this from becoming a junk drawer 🔵

All six areas are **the same three moves**. An outreach list is a board of contact-bits. A submission plan is a board with deadline-bits. An artist statement is a composition. A moodboard is a board of image-bits. Melodies are audio bits on a board.

> **One primitive, everywhere.** No CRM, no submission tracker, no deadline app, no moodboard module. Build **catch → hold → shape** really well and it absorbs the whole creative life.
>
> **If the three moves can express it, that *is* the feature.**

⚑ **This is load-bearing.** It's the only thing standing between "home base for a creative practice" and a junk drawer.

## 2b · ADJACENCY — why the surround claim isn't just "coverage" 🟢

**The owner's example, and it is the whole argument:** *"if you are a writer and you have to find places to publish — well, now you can do that in one place, because you just have a note that you put onto your board, so it's **not detached from your process**."*

**The claim is not that we cover the surround. It's WHERE the surround lives.** In Notion a submission deadline is a row in a table, disconnected from the piece it's about. Here the publications list, the draft, and the references are **in one space**, and the list can be tagged, filtered, and gathered into the pitch.

**That makes it a seam** — arguably the most under-appreciated one: **the seam between the making and the business of making.** Nobody sells it because for everyone else those are two different tools.

⚑ **Priorities implication:** this argues for *generality of material* (more kinds of bit; a table-of-bits view) over more board features — and hard against domain modules.

## 2d · ⭐ THE MISSING DIMENSION: SEQUENCE (surfaced 2026-08-31)

**Four requests, one gap.** The owner has now described the same absence four times without naming it:

| where it appeared | what was missing |
|---|---|
| songwriter — track order on an album | sequence |
| songwriter — a setlist | sequence |
| writer — ordering points for a talk | sequence |
| **a pitch deck** (2026-08-31) | **sequence, over arrangements** |

**The owner's framing of the fourth:** *"pitch deck makes me think we need another composition surface too — like a PowerPoint. **Why the "in between" is exactly right, structurally:**
- a **board** = space, **no order**
- a **composition** = order, **no space** (linear prose)
- a **deck** = **both** — an ordered run of arranged surfaces

That is why creative people reach for Canva/Photoshop over PowerPoint: those are *surfaces you arrange*, that happen to be sequenced.

> **So the gap is not "a presentation feature." The app has NO CONCEPT OF ORDER AT ALL.** One idea sits under all four requests — far better to build than four features.

⭐ **And it is already half-designed:** **document mode** (parked A1 — *a board rendered as a vertical flow*) is precisely "give a board an order." **The deck question and the parked document-mode question are the same question.** ⚑ Re-enter A1 with this evidence: four independent asks, across two personas, is well past the evidence bar that parked it.

## 3 · The concepts

**Material vs surfaces** — the whole app is one move: take scattered material and bring it together into something you can take forward.
- a **bit** is **material** — one thing you caught or made. 🟢 **A unit of capture, not an atomic idea** — "one thing you grabbed," tidy or messy. A rambling multi-topic note-to-self is a fine bit. Never force one-idea-per-bit.
- a **board** and a **composition** are the two **surfaces where you bring material together**.

**What each surface does** 🟡 — described by function, never by what kind of person you are:
- **board** = **a space you hold many things in** — spread out, seen at once, always rearrangeable. *Open and many.*
- **composition** = **one made whole you shape** — a piece with a throughline you form and offer. *Formed and one.*

**Both surfaces are for everyone, in either order.** 🟢 Some scatter on a board then write; others write first and lay it out after. They're **peers you move between** — not step 1 and step 2.

### How a composition differs from a text bit — the unlock

The owner's sharpest question of the session: they're both words, so what's the difference? The answer is **not** words-vs-words:

- a **text bit** is a **fragment you found** — a scrap pulled from the world (a quote, a line, a jotted thought). Raw. Caught.
- a **composition** is a **whole you made** — built, with a shape and a throughline, put together from material. Worked.

**Caught fragment vs. made whole.** A composition is a *made work*; words are just what it happens to be made of. *(A painting is a made whole too. A composition is the made whole that's made of words.)*

### Two framings we tried and moved away from ⚪ *(not retired — under discussion)*

- **"a note is a verbal surface, a board is a spatial surface."** Clean and symmetrical, but it makes the surfaces into personality types. A visual artist writes statements; a writer spreads things on a board. The owner's reaction was **doubt, not rejection**: *"idk if verbal makes sense though like verbal vs spatial?... hm idk."* ⚠ Still written in `model.md` as "a note is a verbal surface" — left there until ruled.
- **"the composition is a step up from the board."** Implies a pipeline. The owner named the counter-case immediately: people who write first and arrange after. More dangerous than the first, because it would teach a false 1-2-3 in onboarding.

## 4 · The edge: the seams 🔵

> **Every other tool makes you stop and switch. We're the one place the whole creative arc flows — and our product is the seams.**

The product is **not any single surface** — it's the **crossings between them.** Everyone else forces a stop: collect here, arrange there, write somewhere else, export, switch. For every competitor those moments are a **hand-off**, so nobody sells them. Genuinely un-owned.

The owner's own image: *"imagine if you could have a Pinterest board that then directly goes into the script that you're writing."*

**What a seam actually is:** a moment where you'd normally have to *stop, leave, and open something else* — and instead you don't. It's not a feature; it's the **absence of a handoff.**

**Seams that already exist in the app:**
- a **hand-doodle sitting beside a written draft** — no other tool lets a drawing and prose live side by side as equals
- a **board of gathered fragments becoming the writing you make from it** (`[[`) — collection feeding creation instead of ending at it
- a **composition placed back onto the board it came from** — the piece returns to its material
- a **board reaching another board** — a hub, so a project can have rooms
- **the same bit living on many boards at once** — one fragment in many constellations, not stuck where you first dropped it

**Seams that don't exist yet but are implied by the surround claim:**
- the **submission list sitting on the board with the work it's for** (the owner's own example — see §2b)
- the **artist statement written next to the pieces it describes**
- the **setlist next to the songs**

**Why nobody sells the seams:** for every competitor, the crossing *is* the exit. Are.na's job ends when you leave to write. Milanote's ends when you export. Notion never had the spatial half to cross from. **You cannot market the moment your user leaves your product** — so the whole category is silent about the exact thing that matters most.

**The craft goes into the seams.** That's the rule for where effort lands: not more capture features, not more board features, but the crossings between modes.

## 5 · The honest competitive read (the owner asked for this specifically — no hype)

Most individual pieces already exist, with entrenched incumbents: catching → Readwise · Are.na · Pinterest. Spatial arranging → Milanote · Muse · Freeform · Miro. Writing → Scrivener · iA Writer · Docs. Links/graph → Obsidian · Roam · Notion.

**"A tool for thought with capture + canvas + writing" is not new.** We shouldn't pretend it is.

### 🟢 ARE.NA IS THE MARKET PRECEDENT (the owner, 2026-08-30)

> *"We have precedent — like Are.na. So many people are using Are.na right now, and even Sublime, but especially Are.na. **I think every creative person has an Are.na.** So I'm like, OK — how do we do that but better, or take that same market?"*

**⚑ Two different competitors doing two different jobs — don't collapse them:**

| | **Are.na** | **Notion** |
|---|---|---|
| what it proves | **the market exists and is reachable** — creative people already adopt a tool like this, and adopt it for its *feeling* | **the feature bar** for structured data |
| how we relate | **take the same audience, do more with it** | good-enough, never head-on (§6) |

**⭐ AND THE SHARPER USE — Are.na as the SWITCHING PITCH, not a persona to serve (the owner, 2026-08-31):**

> *"I don't think we have to go for exactly that Are.na user, but Are.na is a helpful comp for me to think about: OK, people DO want this, and this is what that app does — here's how I want to do it better. I really wanna sell people on the idea: your app does this, but come over here, buddy."*

**Why this matters more than it looks.** It changes the *kind* of pitch we're making. There are two ways to sell a tool like this:
- **Explain a new category** — "it's a home base for a creative practice, where material flows into…" → expensive, abstract, and you lose people before the second clause.
- **Argue a switch from something they already use** — *"you already do this in Are.na. Here's the same thing, except it finishes."* → cheap, concrete, and **the person has already decided they want the behavior.**

**The second is far easier, and Are.na is the only comp where it works** — because Are.na users have already self-selected into "I collect things beautifully, on purpose." No education needed; the entire pitch is the delta.

**The delta, in one line each** *(🔵 raw seeds — ⚑ the owner writes the actual words)*: your blocks just sit there → here they feed the writing · you leave to make anything → here you don't leave · your channels are flat → here you arrange in space, by hand · you collect → **you finish**.

⚠ **The discipline this implies:** a switching pitch is only honest if the switch is actually good. Every gap the persona passes surface (`product-concept-people.md`) is a place the switch would disappoint someone — so the pitch can't ship ahead of the build. **This is a reason to finish level 1 properly, not a reason to skip it.**

⚪ **Still open, and it bites here:** Are.na is social; we are not. A switcher leaving Are.na leaves *being seen* behind. → `product-concept-privacy.md`.

**Why Are.na is the right precedent and not just a competitor:** it proves the hardest thing — that creative people will adopt, love and stay in a beautiful single-purpose tool with no growth mechanics. What it can't do is **finish anything**: you collect, and then you leave to write. That's the exact gap this app is for.

⚪ **Open, and it matters:** Are.na is **social** — channels are public, people follow and re-block each other's finds, and a meaningful part of why people stay is being *seen*. This app is single-resident with no publish act. **If we're taking Are.na's market, are we taking it without the thing that makes Are.na sticky?** → straight into `product-concept-privacy.md`. This is the sharpest open question in the whole workstream.

### ⚪ "Why does Notion win on flexibility — what do they do that we can't?" (the owner's challenge, 2026-08-30)

A fair push, and the first answer was too loose. Honestly:

**What the owner is right about:** **Notion has no freeform spatial canvas at all.** Its "board" is kanban columns — no placing something where it belongs, no pen, no doodles, no mixed media in space. **On breadth of MODE we win, and it isn't close.**

**What Notion actually wins:** **structured data.** Typed properties (date · select · status · person · URL), filtering and sorting by them, multiple views of one dataset, relations and rollups between databases. Plus deep nesting and templates. That is their engine, and we have none of it.

**The honest verdict:** *breadth of mode → us. Depth of structure → Notion.* We should not try to close that gap; §6's rules say good-enough, not best. ⚠ **But see §2c** — the tables idea aims straight at it, so this is a live tension, not a settled one.

**Where the edge actually lives — an intersection, not a feature:**
1. **The whole arc, in one home.** Are.na collects but you leave to write. Obsidian writes but the spatial side is bolted on — *"notes-faced,"* the owner's word. Milanote and Muse arrange but the finished thing is weak. Few carry you scatter → arrange → made-thing without switching, and fewer let you move **both directions**.
2. **The feeling — an artist's private studio.** Calm, single-person, beautiful, no algorithm, no engagement-farming, no AI slop. Are.na proved creatives will love a tool for its feeling alone — but Are.na can't finish anything. A studio that's beautiful **and** finishes is open.

### The caveats — kept in deliberately

- **This is mostly potential, not realized.** The cut-across is only partly built. The claim has to be earned by the build.
- **We're late to a crowded space.** People stay in Notion and Obsidian out of habit, not love.
- **"Single-player, no AI" is a real positioning risk** in an AI-hyped market. We'd be choosing to be the calm anti-tool — and we'd have to mean it.

## 6 · Cut-across, with discipline

The owner's play is to **cut across** all these areas, not beat a specialist head-on. That's a real position — you won't out-link Obsidian or out-collect Pinterest, and shouldn't try.

*(Claude first overstated this as "if we compete on features, we lose." The owner pushed back correctly: each incumbent is a **specialist**, and the play was never to beat one head-on.)*

But **"best of each, in one" is a known graveyard**, and it's worth being specific about *how* it kills a tool rather than just asserting it. The failure runs in a predictable order: you add a feature to match a competitor → the feature needs settings → the settings need a settings screen → the surface gets busier → the calm goes → and the thing people actually loved (that it felt good to be in) is gone, traded for a feature nobody switched for. **Notion is the endpoint of that road, and Notion is not beautiful.** The specific loss for us would be the aesthetic track — the quiet, luminous, unhurried surface — which is not decoration here. It's **half the edge**, and it's the half Are.na proved people will adopt a tool for on its own.

**The three rules that make cut-across win instead of sprawl:**
1. **Good enough in each area** — not best, not trying to be.
2. **Great at the seams** — that's where the craft goes.
3. **A ruthless no** to feature-matching that bloats or clutters.

## 7 · What we deliberately do NOT do

- **No domain modules** — no CRM, no submission tracker, no deadline app, no calendar.
- **No feature-matching** a specialist to look competitive.
- **No AI-written user-facing voice** — Claude supplies principles and raw seeds; the owner writes the words.
- **The never-list holds:** collaboration · AI · analytics · audience machines. One resident per home.
- **No forced pipeline** — the arc is an invitation, never a mandate.
