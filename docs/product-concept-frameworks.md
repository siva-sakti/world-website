## Why this file exists

The frameworks aren't decoration. They do three jobs at once:
1. **Validate** that the model matches how creative work actually happens (the owner asked directly: *"is there research on this… do people's creative processes work this way?"*).
2. **Supply shared language** — vocabulary a designer or artist already owns, so we're *naming something they already feel* rather than teaching a new system. That's market fit and onboarding in one move.
3. **Bound features** — a good framework says what *not* to build as clearly as what to build.

---

## 1 · Diverge ↔ converge 🟡 — the one that landed

**What it is:** the oldest and most-tested model of creative thinking (Guilford; the backbone of design's **Double Diamond**). Creative minds swing between two modes — **diverge**: open up, gather, branch, hold many possibilities. **converge**: select, connect, shape into one thing. Critically it's a **cycle, not a line**: diverge, converge, diverge again.

**The map** 🔵 — three positions, not two. *(The owner felt the wrinkle themselves: "spreading on a board can be called diverging." Correct, and it produced the better map.)*

| | what it is | on the cycle |
|---|---|---|
| **bit** | material you caught | **divergent** — you widen; catch a possibility |
| **board** | a space you hold many things in | **converges without locking** — you cluster, arrange, structure, and nothing commits; re-spreadable forever |
| **composition** | one made whole you shape | **the surface of convergence** |

**The board's whole power is convergence you can always undo.** That's why it feels divergent even as you arrange it — the one surface that lets you impose structure and keep everything open.

**The strategic payoff:** a designer instantly reads the board as the exploration-and-synthesis space — the messy productive middle of the Double Diamond — and the composition as the deliverable. Nothing has to be taught.

**The owner's philosophical line, in their words:** *"we actually accommodate the process of getting from divergent to convergent — and usually all these interfaces are very notes-faced."*

⚠ **Caveat we owe ourselves:** we have not looked for the *critiques* of this model. If the pitch rests on it, we should know its weaknesses. → question 6 below.

## 2 · The extended mind 🔵

**What it is:** Clark & Chalmers — thinking doesn't only happen in your head; it happens **out in space**, on the table, on the wall.

**What it justified:** the owner's instinct that a bit shouldn't get its own isolated screen — *"my Apple note actually covers a bunch of topics and ideas, but I don't wanna see it on its own interface, I wanna see it as part of this other world."*

**Promoted from instinct to design principle: context over isolation.** A bit's meaning is mostly *what sits around it*; pulling it onto a lone page strips the thing that made it matter. → show bits among other things, rarely alone.

## 3 · Recombination / bisociation 🔵

**What it is:** Koestler — new things come from **connecting fragments**.

**What it does for us, in both directions:** it says connection is genuinely load-bearing, **and** it bounds the feature. **Placing things near each other on a board is already connecting them** — proximity and clusters mean "these relate." Drawn lines only add something when a link must be **explicit and directional** ("this caused that").

→ **arrangement is the everyday connection; drawn lines stay a rare power-move.** This is why the steady stream of line-features keeps getting declined. *(Feeds the open question in `product-concept-promise.md` §8.2.)*

## 4 · Practice examples — the same shape across fields

Offered earlier in the session, on the owner's *"is there ground to stand on here?"*, and they landed:

- **Twyla Tharp's box** — every project starts with a literal box; she throws in everything (notes, clippings, objects, music) and makes the dance **from the box**. Board → composition, exactly.
- **Mood boards** in fashion and design — scattered references → a synthesized direction. Universal.
- **Bowie's cut-ups** — fragments rearranged into songs.
- **Commonplace books** — for centuries, writers copied quotes and scraps into a personal book, then composed from it.

**Different fields, one shape:** a private scatter you work from, then a made thing you offer out.

⚪ **Not verified.** These were recalled, not sourced. If any goes into user-facing copy it needs a real citation first.

## 5 · ⚪ THE RESEARCH ROUND — draft questions, to sharpen together

**The owner's process rule:** *"we have to think to define the question and then send it off."* So these are **drafts to argue with**, not a brief. The house rule from experience: **specific answerable questions, never a broad "compile everything" sweep.**

| # | question | why it earns its place |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **What named frameworks describe the gather-then-make motion in the fields creative people actually train in** — art school, design, music, writing pedagogy? We have Double Diamond, Tharp's box, commonplace books. What else has a **name a practitioner would recognise**? | The whole value is *shared language*. A framework nobody's heard of does none of the three jobs. |
| 2 | **Is there a named account of "structure you can always undo"** — provisional, reversible, non-committal organisation? *(Candidates to check: Schön's reflective practice / "backtalk of the situation"; Kirsh & Maglio's epistemic vs. pragmatic action; "loose fit" in design.)* | This is **the board's specific power** and currently our own coinage. If it has a real name, the board gets much easier to explain. |
| 3 | **What's the strongest named account of why externalising helps thinking**, and which has vocabulary creatives already use — extended mind, distributed cognition (Hutchins), epistemic action? | We're using extended mind; it may not be the most legible one to this audience. |
| 4 | **Is there any named account of the *surround* of creative work** — the non-making labour (statements, outreach, opportunities, presentation)? Artists' "invisible work," practice management, career labour in the arts? | **The newest and least-supported claim in the whole positioning** (`promise.md` §2). If nothing names it, that's worth knowing — it might mean it's un-owned, or it might mean it isn't real. |
| 5 | **Where does the arc actually break in practice** — what do practitioner accounts say about where the gather→make handoff fails, and what do people currently do instead? | Tests **the seams claim** empirically instead of asserting it. |
| 6 | **What is the serious critique of diverge/converge and the Double Diamond?** | The owner asked for honest, not hype. If the pitch rests on this frame we should know where it's weak **before** a smart user does. |

### ⭐ ADDED 2026-08-31 — the research round is now TWO questions wide

The owner's gallerist example (`product-concept-promise.md` §Required artifacts) changed the brief. It isn't only about creative *frameworks* any more. **Question 7 may matter more than 1–6:**

| # | question | why |
|---|---|---|
| **7** | **Per discipline — what documents is a practitioner actually REQUIRED to produce that are not their art, and how do they make them today?** (artist: list of work · statement · CV. writer: pitch · query · bio. musician: one-sheet · EPK. designer: portfolio · case study. filmmaker: treatment · shot list.) | **Directly buildable.** Names real artifacts, real formats, and the real tool being suffered today — which is the switching pitch, per discipline. And it is the one thing Claude **must not invent**: the owner is the oracle for one discipline only; every other workflow Claude writes unaided is plausible fiction. |

**Sources that count for Q7:** practitioner writing · how-to videos and guides for that discipline · gallery/publisher/label submission requirements (these are *published* and authoritative) · real people who can be asked. **Not** Claude's model of how a fashion designer works.

**Suggested shape when we run it:** one focused pass per question, sources named, findings marked *usable in copy* vs *internal only*. Not one big sweep.
