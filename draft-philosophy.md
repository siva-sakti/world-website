# Philosophy

*The **why** behind this project. `SPEC.md` is the **what**, built to serve this. `CLAUDE.md` is **how we work**. If the spec ever contradicts this document, that's a flag to raise, not a decision to make silently. This is a living draft.*

## The one idea

I take in far more than I keep. Ideas I've had, things I've read and seen — I forget them too quickly, and I almost never go back. I want a place that helps me **nurture, revisit, and grow my own thinking over time**, so I build *deeply* on what I already understand instead of endlessly consuming new things and losing them.

Not a place to *store* thoughts. A place to *grow* them — where an idea is planted, returned to, connected to others, and slowly develops, instead of being captured once and never seen again.

To do that I need two things I've never had together: the **spatial freedom of paper** (doodle, diagram, lay things out where they belong) and the **memory of digital** (search it, link it, never lose it). Handwriting and typing on the same page; a wealth of information I can actually find my way back into.

It is a place to think, and to *keep* thinking. It is not a product.

## Why the tools I already have don't do this

- **Paper** gives me spatial freedom and my own hand, but nothing is searchable, linkable, or backed up.
- **Obsidian / Notion** give me search, links, and structure, but not *my* layout — I can't drag a doodle into the corner, wrap a paragraph around a sketch, and pin a photo where it belongs. The page is theirs, not mine.

I want the connectedness of a linked notebook and the freedom of a blank sheet of paper — at the same time.

## Everything is a canvas

There aren't different kinds of thing — there's one: a **canvas**, a space where I place **bits** (a line of text, a doodle, a photo, a voice memo, a link) wherever they belong to me. A canvas can be a single line caught on a walk or a full composed page — the same thing at different degrees of filled-in. Spatial placement isn't decoration on a note; it *is* how I think, and how I organise. Things get their places, and from there the flow finds itself.

And every bit is its own taggable, dated thing — so the same doodle or line can live in its canvas *and* surface, recombined by meaning, whenever I pull the tag it belongs to. My thoughts stay re-combinable, instead of stuck wherever I first dropped them.

## How thoughts connect

Two mechanisms:

- **Tags** — what something is *about*. Tapped from what already exists, never typed as syntax. On every bit, and on the canvas itself.
- **Links** — one thing pointing at another.

From these come **backlinks** ("what points at this?") and a **graph** — the whole web of my thinking, made visible. The knowledge is a connected web, not a pile.

## Returning is the point

Capturing a thought is the easy half. The hard half — the half most tools skip — is **coming back to it.** But I don't need the tool to nag me or quiz me like homework. I need it to be a place I *want* to return to on my own: a surface that feels good to my eyes, where my thinking quietly accumulates, so I can wander back through it the way I used to wander back through my own Tumblr feed. Revisiting should happen as a natural side effect of *a place worth visiting* — not as a chore a review system assigns me.

(Someday, a gentle nudge might help — a random old fragment surfaced, a topic worth another look. Not now, and never as the main event.)

## The gradient — from raw to formed, from private to shared

A thought travels. It starts raw and private — a morning page, a reflection meant for no one. From that raw material I pull **fragments**: smaller, a little more formed. Fragments get composed into **pieces**: the most formed, the most shareable. The system holds *every stage of that journey in one place*, so nothing has to leave home to grow up.

Two axes move roughly together but are not the same thing:

- **Maturity** — seed → seedling → plant → fruit.
- **Exposure** — *private* (only me) → *shared* (people I hand the key to) → *public* (open web).

**New things start private.** Publishing is a deliberate act — bringing a thought outward, on purpose. Nothing is exposed by accident. The morning pages stay mine unless I choose otherwise.

## How it should feel

- **Capture is instant.** If it's slow on my phone, it's broken.
- **Composition is unhurried and spatial.** Playing with a page is a sit-down pleasure, on a real screen.
- **I see my handwriting and my typing on the same page.**
- **Everything is findable** — searchable, tagged, linked. That includes the pieces, not just the fragments.
- **I own all of it.** I can export everything, always.
- **The look is mine.** The visual identity is hand-drawn, added by me later. Until then the build stays plain — unstyled and working beats styled and guessed.
- **It's a home, not a platform.** One resident — me, the only one who writes. A few trusted people can be handed a key to *look*. No collaboration, no audience, no growth mechanics.

## In and out

**Welcome:** the free-canvas piece; fragment-to-fragment links; a graph view; pieces that are as findable as fragments.

**Not this:** multiple *writers*, collaboration, AI features, analytics, anything built for an audience. Sharing means handing a trusted person the key to *look* — never opening the doors to the world, never running a product.

## How I reach it

A web app I can install on my phone and my desktop (a PWA), so it feels like *my* app, not a website I visit. **Capture from the phone; compose on the desktop.**
