# Vision & language — what this app is, in words that work

**What this is:** the language book — how we talk about this app, to ourselves and to other people. Drafted by Claude from the owner's own words (2026-08-25 session); **the owner rewrites anything here into her own voice — this doc is raw material, not scripture.** Rule for every line: literal, grounded, concrete. If a sentence could hang in an art gallery but couldn't be said across a kitchen table, it doesn't belong here.

---

## 1 · What it is, plainly

A personal app where everything you collect and think lives in one place — images, quotes, doodles, voice notes, half-thoughts, and finished writing — and where you can actually *work* with it: arrange it on visual boards, tag it, link it into your writing, and grow raw material into finished pieces. One person per home. Built to be beautiful enough that returning to it is a pleasure, and organized enough that nothing gets lost.

## 2 · Who it's for: the multi-mind

The person with many interests running at once — astrology *and* economics *and* Buddhist philosophy *and* design. Most tools quietly punish this person: every interest needs its own folder, app, or account, and the whole never meets. This app is built for the **multi-**: multi-interest, multi-domain, multimedia, multi-mode. Concretely:

- **The visual person** gets boards — arrange images, fragments, and doodles spatially, like a wall.
- **The knowledge person** gets tags, search, links, and a graph — everything findable and connected.
- **The writer** gets notes — real pages to write on, that can pull any collected fragment into a sentence.
- The same person is usually all three on different days. Nothing makes them choose.

## 3 · The problem, taught three concrete ways

**For the consumer-of-things:** you read, watch, and screenshot constantly. Where did it all go? Camera roll, twelve apps, nowhere. You keep consuming instead of building because nothing you take in has a place to accumulate. → Here, everything lands in one home, and *stays workable* — not a pile, a workshop.

**For the Are.na / moodboard person:** you love collecting and arranging — but your blocks just sit there. You can't *write* with them, can't grow a channel into an essay. → Here, a board's fragments can be gathered straight into a piece of writing. Collection feeds creation instead of ending at it.

**For the Obsidian / notes person:** you have links and structure — but the page is rigid text. You can't drag a doodle to the corner, pin a photo where it belongs, arrange thoughts in space. → Here, spatial freedom and linked memory are the same app.

## 4 · The journey of a thought (the gradient, literally)

The founding idea: **raw → fragments → pieces.** A concrete run of it:

1. On a walk you dictate a **raw** thought into the quick-jot.
2. Later you clip a quote from a book and screenshot a diagram — **bits**, tagged, sourced.
3. On the *rebirth* board you arrange the jot, the quote, the diagram — a shape emerges.
4. In ✎ write you start a **piece** — a real page — and type `[[` to pull the quote and the diagram into your sentences.
5. The piece is now a **note**: titled, dated, findable, sitting beside your boards — and every fragment in it still knows where it came from.

Nothing left home to grow up. That's the whole product in five steps.

## 5 · The feeling promise: inspiration × clarity

The two things people usually trade off. Moodboard tools feel inspiring and chaotic; databases feel clear and dead. The promise here is the intersection — **all of the inspiration, none of the chaos** — and the design carries half of it: calm, organic, beautiful surfaces (worth screenshotting), on top of a strict, boring, reliable data model (nothing lost, everything findable, export always).

## 6 · Positioning, one line each

- **vs Are.na:** Are.na collects; it doesn't help you write, grow, or finish. This does.
- **vs Sublime:** same family — collection-first, creation-later. Here creation is a first-class room, not a maybe.
- **vs Obsidian:** Obsidian links text; it doesn't give you walls, space, or your own hand. This does both.
- **vs Notion:** Notion is a database wearing a document. This is a studio wearing a notebook.
- **vs paper:** paper has the freedom and the hand — and loses everything. This keeps the freedom and remembers.
